# Protecting your app idea

*Notes from a conversation on Sep 5, 2026 — not legal advice. Consult an actual IP attorney before anything with real money or disclosure on the line.*

## The core fact

An idea in the abstract, like "a resume-building app with AI," is not something the law protects. Nobody can own that concept, and a well-funded competitor can legally build something similar under a different name no matter what protections are in place. What the law actually protects are the concrete things you create around the idea, and what usually stops copying in practice has more to do with business structure and execution speed than with law.

## What's actually protectable

Your source code, written content, and design assets are covered automatically by copyright the moment you create them, no registration required, though registering with the U.S. Copyright Office strengthens your ability to sue for damages if someone copies your actual code or text.

The name "ResumeLingo" and any logo can be protected by trademark. This stops someone from causing customer confusion with a similar brand, but it does not stop someone from building a similar product under a different name.

A genuinely novel technical method, not just "AI resume builder" as a concept but a specific new mechanism, could in principle be patented. Software patents are expensive, slow, and narrow in practice, and most SaaS ideas don't clear the novelty and non-obviousness bar required.

Trade secret law protects things deliberately kept confidential, such as a scoring algorithm's exact weighting or customer and pricing data, but only for as long as the information is actually kept secret. Once it leaks or becomes public, the protection is gone.

## What actually stops most idea theft in practice

Requiring an NDA before showing the idea to a contractor, co-founder, or investor. Putting IP-assignment clauses in every contractor or employee agreement, so their work product is unambiguously owned by the business rather than by them personally. Keeping implementation details, prompts, and admin tooling out of public view rather than in a widely circulated pitch deck.

Beyond legal paperwork, the real moat is execution: being first to market, building the brand and user base, accumulating data and customer relationships, and iterating faster than a copier can. Legal protections mainly defend against someone taking your specific code, content, brand, or confidential material, not against someone independently building a competing idea.

## When to bring in a real attorney

Worth a paid consultation (not necessarily a retainer) once you're approaching fundraising, hiring, or a partnership where real disclosure is about to happen. An IP attorney can assess whether anything here is genuinely patentable and draft NDAs or contractor IP-assignment agreements that will actually hold up.

## Practical next steps if useful

Adding a copyright notice and a proper Terms of Service to the app. Drafting an NDA or contractor IP-assignment template for outside legal review.
